In late 1942, General Leslie Groves selected theoretical physicist J. Robert Oppenheimer to lead Project Y, the ultra-secret weapon laboratory of the Manhattan Project.

High atop the isolated Pajarito Plateau in New Mexico, Los Alamos quickly grew from a rustic ranch school into a bustling clandestine city of world-renowned scientists.

Thousands of miles to the east in Oak Ridge, Tennessee, the gargantuan K-25 gaseous diffusion plant consumed vast electrical power to separate rare Uranium-235.

Meanwhile along the Columbia River in Hanford, Washington, massive industrial facilities including the B-Reactor produced the world's first plutonium.

By July 1945, scientists assembled the Gadget test device inside the master bedroom of the abandoned McDonald Ranch house in the New Mexico desert.

At 5:29 AM on July 16, 1945, the Trinity test detonated with the force of twenty-one kilotons of TNT, vaporizing the steel tower and turning desert sand into green trinitite glass.

Watching the towering mushroom cloud rise from a bunker miles away, Oppenheimer later recalled the Hindu scripture line: Now I am become Death, the destroyer of worlds.

Just three weeks later on the Pacific island of Tinian, ground crews loaded the atomic weapon Little Boy into the bomb bay of the B-29 Superfortress Enola Gay.

On the morning of August 6, 1945, the Enola Gay dropped its payload over Hiroshima, unleashing unprecedented devastation and ushering humanity into the nuclear age.

Finally on September 2, 1945, Allied and Japanese officials gathered aboard the battleship USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay to sign the formal surrender documents, bringing World War II to an end.
